## 2026-08-20: Benchmark pass, corrected an internal coherence error and re-based financials on real B2B SaaS data

Neyda asked for the dataset design to be checked against real-world conditions and internal coherence before treating Phase 1 as locked. This surfaced one real bug and prompted a full recheck of the financial assumptions.

**The bug:** the original data model (both the initial draft of `data_model.md` and this phase's handoff prompt) specified 600 won deals and 600 lost deals out of 1,200 closed opportunities, an even 50/50 split. That implies a 50 percent aggregate win rate. The win rates already locked for the same dataset (Enterprise 28 percent pre-trigger / 14 percent post, Mid-Market 30 percent, SMB 35 percent, Startup 5 percent) top out at 35 percent for any single segment. A 50 percent blended win rate was mathematically impossible given those inputs; the two locked assumptions contradicted each other.

**The fix:** kept the total closed-deal count (1,200) and total opportunity count (1,500) as already confirmed, and derived a segment mix of closed attempts (Enterprise 150, Mid-Market 350, SMB 575, Startup 125) that, combined with the existing win rates, produces a blended 28.3 percent win rate, in line with published B2B averages (roughly 19-21 percent industry-wide, higher for an SMB-weighted portfolio like this one). This yields 340 won deals and 860 lost deals, replacing the 600/600 placeholder everywhere it appeared (`case_study_scenario.md`, `data_model.md`, the `customers` table row count, and `generate_data.py`'s SEGMENTS config). Full arithmetic is reproducible from `/tmp/coherence_check.py` (session scratch file, not saved to the repo; rerun the same logic if this needs re-deriving).

**Benchmarks used** (Optifai's 939-company win-rate study, Landbase's 2026 win-rate benchmarks, getaleph's 2026 CAC payback benchmarks, Optifai's churn benchmarks, and ICONIQ's 2026 GTM benchmark report via Blossom Street Ventures' summary; see sources below):
- Win rate: SMB 30-40%, Enterprise (>$100k ACV) 12-18%, blended average ~19-21%. Real-world 2024-to-2025 industry data (Ebsta/Pavilion) shows win rates compressing from 29% to 19% year over year, a similar direction and rough magnitude to this scenario's 28%-to-14% Enterprise decline, which supports the story's plausibility even though ours is somewhat more severe by design.
- CAC payback: SMB under 12 months (best-in-class under 6), Mid-Market 14-18 months, Enterprise 18-24 months (lower-ACV enterprise, $50k-100k, closer to a 22-month median). Locked Enterprise payback (13mo baseline to 26mo post-decline) sits below the published enterprise range at baseline, which is defensible since Anchorpoint's Enterprise tier starts at $50k, blurring into upper-mid-market, and the post-decline figure exceeds the published range's ceiling, which is the point: the crisis state is genuinely outside normal bounds, not just a bad quarter.
- Churn: SMB 22-32% gross annual, Mid-Market 8-14%, Enterprise 5-10%. Locked at Enterprise 8%, Mid-Market 11%, SMB 27%, Startup 38% (extrapolated above the SMB range since Startup is an even smaller, less committed tier).
- S&M spend: combined sales and marketing typically 27-45% of ARR for venture-backed SaaS. Locked at $3.6M annual S&M against a recomputed ~$7.2M ARR, about 50%, above the typical range but explainable and on-narrative: it is elevated specifically because of the underperforming Enterprise investment, which is the CFO's actual complaint in this scenario, not an unexplained anomaly.
- AE quota: median enterprise AE new-business quota ~$800k/year (Bridge Group 2024). Cross-checked against 4 Enterprise AEs and 28 won Enterprise deals over 20 months at ~$95k average deal size (~$133k ARR/AE/year against target): well under quota, consistent with a genuinely underperforming, newly-staffed enterprise team rather than a healthy one.

**Also corrected:** the original scenario draft conflated "$450,000 monthly net cash burn" with the company's total burn, but that figure did not reconcile with the stated 19-month runway from a $22M raise ($22M / $450k/mo implies roughly 49 months of runway, not 19). ARR was also recomputed bottom-up from the corrected 340 won deals, segment deal sizes, and segment churn rates (assuming an average ~10-month tenure across a 20-month window), landing at approximately $7.2M rather than the earlier unreconciled $6.2M placeholder.

## 2026-08-20: Phase 1.5 redesign, generator rebuilt on causal mechanisms

An external plan review (`docs/plan_review.md`) rated the plan 5.5 out of 10 against its own stated goal and found one foundational problem plus five hard blockers. Neyda authorized challenging the premise and reopening Phase 1. This entry records the resulting decisions. Several supersede earlier entries above; the earlier entries stand as written, per this log's rules.

**Root finding: the dataset was generated from the answer.** `win_rate_pre: 0.28` and `win_rate_post: 0.14` were literal config values in `src/generate_data.py`, and `docs/locked_business_questions.md` stated every expected result to the specific number before any data existed. Phase 3 could therefore only recover the parameters Phase 2 was handed, which demonstrates data generation rather than analysis. Because reproducibility is an advertised selling point, any reader opening the generator would have seen the finding hardcoded, and the case study's central claim would not have survived that.

**Decision 1: encode causes, never outcomes.** No win rate parameter exists anywhere in the redesigned generator. A deal is won when the rep engages the full buying group before the buyer loses patience, then beats the competitor. Parameters are buying group size (Gartner, 6 to 10 stakeholders in complex B2B), buyer timeout (enterprise cycles 6 to 9 months), rep touch rate, and new AE ramp (Bridge Group, 9 to 12 months for enterprise, with a below-parity ceiling for a rep who never receives enablement). Every parameter is a published benchmark about buyer or rep behaviour; none is a benchmark about win rate.

**Decision 2: the trigger date is deleted, not moved.** This also fixes a coherence bug the review surfaced. The Phase 1 scenario said all four Enterprise AEs were inexperienced from January 2025 while claiming win rate held at 28 percent until July. A cause in January cannot produce a step change in July. Resolved correctly: `ENTERPRISE_PUSH_DATE = 2025-01-01` is a staffing event with no performance effect attached. Because enterprise cycles run six to nine months, deals closing in early 2025 were opened in 2024 under the founder-led motion, so any decline surfaces in closed-won data around mid-2025 for a real reason. The lag between cause and visible symptom is now itself a finding (Question 3) and is arguably the most transferable insight in the project.

**Decision 3: Enterprise volume raised from 150 to 420 closed attempts, window extended to 26 months.** At 150, with the window's actual 6-month-pre / 14-month-post split, the headline comparison tested at p = 0.062 with a 95 percent CI of [-0.7pp, +28.7pp], crossing zero. The central claim of the case study was not statistically supportable. At 420 with a realistic 25/75 volume ramp, the same comparison tests near p = 0.001. Total closed deals go 1,200 to 2,200, pipeline 300 to 400, companies 500 to 900, window 20 to 26 months (2024-07-01 to 2026-08-31) so a genuine pre-push baseline sits inside it.

**Decision 4: Startup segment cut.** Supersedes the 2026-08-20 decision locking four segments. At any realistic volume it produced too few wins to support analysis, contributed under half a percent of ARR, and was already excluded from the payback work. Attempts redistributed across the three remaining segments.

**Decision 5: three tables added.** `sales_reps`, because rep capability is the independent variable of the entire analysis and in Phase 1 the team existed only as a comment in the generator, making the central hypothesis untestable from exported data. `deal_stage_history`, because the Phase 1 schema carried a single scalar `days_in_stage` on a table whose only stages were Closed Won and Closed Lost, so the locked question about where deals stall had no underlying data at all. `sm_spend`, monthly rows replacing an annual scalar, because dividing an annual figure across a multi-month window made period-correct CAC impossible.

**Decision 6: gross margin locked at 78 percent.** Previously undocumented while silently determining every payback figure in the scenario; reverse-engineering the Phase 1 numbers showed they implied exactly 80 percent. Locked at 78 percent against the B2B SaaS median of 75 to 80 percent (Orb 2025, SaaS Capital 2026).

**Decision 7: LTV capped at 3 years, naive 1/churn kept only as a labelled contrast.** At 8 percent Enterprise churn the naive method implies a 12.5-year customer life and produces an Enterprise LTV:CAC near 5.7:1, comfortably above the 3:1 rule of thumb. The struggling segment would have passed the single most-cited unit economics test, and a reader would reasonably have concluded the company should keep spending, destroying the case study's recommendation. The 3-year cap yields roughly 1.4:1. Showing both figures and explaining why the capped one is defensible is now a deliberate design decision and a highlighted judgment call rather than a hidden assumption.

**Decision 8: four competing explanations added, one partially real.** EU competitive entry in Q3 2025 genuinely takes EU Enterprise deals and accounts for a minority of the decline. The August 2025 price increase, seasonality, and Partnerships channel growth are red herrings that look plausible and are ruled out by specific cuts. Phase 3 does not know which is which and must quantify each. Making one confounder genuinely contributory is deliberate: an analyst who reports "I ruled out three things and all were zero" has demonstrated less than one who finds a real contributing cause and correctly sizes it as partial.

**Decision 9: findings may not be written into locked documents.** Standing rule for all remaining phases. `docs/locked_business_questions.md` has had every "What the answer will show" section deleted and replaced with what the question tests, what would falsify it, and the minimum rigor the answer must meet. Results go in `docs/findings_summary.md` after measurement and nowhere before. Confidence intervals are required on every headline win-rate comparison, and Enterprise cuts are quarterly grain only, since monthly Enterprise volume cannot support a readable chart.

**Decision 10: data wins over narrative.** If Phase 2 output contradicts a scenario constant, the scenario document changes to match the data, never the reverse. A plausibility band (18 to 34 percent for the experienced Enterprise motion, 6 to 20 percent for the new-team period) replaces the former hard targets. Results inside the band are accepted and recorded as-is.

**Decision 11: both seeds set.** Phase 1 seeded stdlib `random` only while planning numpy draws, so the advertised byte-identical reproducibility would have silently failed. A single module-level `np.random.default_rng(SEED)` is now the only source of randomness in the generator.

**Documented limitation, deliberately not engineered away.** At 8 percent annual churn with roughly 60 to 80 Enterprise customers observed for an average of about 13 months, the dataset will contain roughly five observed Enterprise churn events. Enterprise retention cannot be measured from this data at any volume achievable inside a realistic window. Phase 3 must use benchmark churn with a stated sensitivity range and say so plainly rather than reporting a measured retention curve off five events. This goes in the honesty layer as a demonstration of judgment.

**Financial re-base.** The larger dataset makes Anchorpoint a roughly $14M ARR company rather than $7.2M, so the Phase 1 benchmark pass was redone rather than discarded: Series B $45M (from $22M), total burn ~$1.25M/month giving ~18 months runway, S&M $6.2M/year at 43 percent of ARR (inside the 27 to 45 percent band), Enterprise team 8 AEs rather than 4 so attempts per AE stays realistic at the new volume. Calibration runs put Enterprise AE output near 30 percent of the $800k Bridge Group median quota, consistent with a genuinely underperforming newly staffed team.

## 2026-08-20: Agent handoff pack and validation architecture

Neyda asked for a prompt set making this build transferable to another agent cold. Four forks confirmed before writing: receiving agent starts at Phase 1.5 implementation, hard phase gates with her sign-off between every phase, a standalone validation script, and the EA task board path.

**Decision 12: hard phase gates, no auto-proceed.** The agent completes a phase, runs validation, logs, reports, and stops. Considered auto-proceeding on green validation for speed. Rejected: this is a portfolio piece carrying Neyda's name, and a wrong finding written into prose is expensive to unwind. Green validation proves structural integrity, not that a finding is one she wants published.

**Decision 13: validation is a standalone re-runnable script, not only an inline `validate()` call.** `src/validate_dataset.py`, run after every phase. Rationale: Phase 3 transforms the data, and transformations introduce their own bugs (a merge dropping lost deals, a filter removing rows, a groupby collapsing the segment dimension). A check that only runs at generation time cannot see any of it. The script re-reads CSVs from disk each run, so it catches drift introduced later. Six tiers: T0 structural, T1 referential, T2 reconciliation, T3 logical, T4 statistical plausibility, T5 anti-regression.

**Decision 14: T4 reports and never blocks.** Deliberate asymmetry. T0-T3 and T5 are hard failures that block a phase. T4 (win rates in band, deal size means, power, censoring smoke test) only warns. If a statistical check could block a phase, the path of least resistance becomes editing data until it passes, which is precisely the failure this project was rebuilt to remove. A T4 warning means investigate the mechanism, and the phase prompts say so explicitly.

**Decision 15: T5 exists as an automated tripwire against outcome encoding.** The Phase 1 defect is easy to reintroduce under time pressure and invisible in the output, since the numbers still look fine. T5 scans the generator for `win_rate` dict keys, `win_rate_pre/post` assignments, and `PROBLEM_TRIGGER_DATE`, and scans the locked docs for answer-prediction phrases. Doc scanning starts at the first question or scenario section so the Phase 1.5 rewrite notes, which legitimately quote the old heading while explaining its deletion, do not trip it. Verified with a negative control: injecting `"win_rate_post": 0.14` caused a hard failure and exit code 1; removing it restored a clean pass at 13 checks, 0 failures.

**Decision 16: the Phase 2 "aha-moment sanity check" instruction is void.** The Phase 1 handoff instructed the agent to confirm the Enterprise divergence "shows up cleanly" and to treat its absence as a blocker. That is outcome verification, not validation, and following it would recreate the original defect. Replaced in V2 with: run `report_emergent_outcomes()`, compare against the plausibility band, record whatever it says. Inside the band, accept and record. Outside, investigate the mechanism, never adjust a parameter toward a preferred number. A confidence interval crossing zero is a genuine blocker to flag, not to paper over.

**OPEN QUESTION, requires Neyda's ruling before Phase 2: no contemporaneous experienced-rep control group.** All eight Enterprise AEs share a hire date, so `has_enterprise_experience` is perfectly confounded with calendar period. Every inexperienced-rep deal is also a post-push deal. Question 5 asks whether rep capability explains the decline, and as designed the analysis cannot separate a rep effect from a time effect; anything else that changed in 2025 is an equally valid explanation.

Recommended amendment: the two founder-led experienced reps continue to carry roughly 10 to 15 percent of post-push Enterprise deals. Realistic (a founder does not stop taking enterprise calls the day a team is hired), cheap (a small change to rep assignment in `generate_deals()`), and it turns the single cleanest test in the case study from heavily caveated into a clean within-period comparison.

Not implemented. If Neyda declines, Phase 3 must state the confounding explicitly and Question 5's answer must be caveated rather than asserted. Note the partial mitigation available either way: showing the effect is Enterprise-specific while Mid-Market and SMB span the same period without deterioration rules out company-wide time effects, though it does not isolate rep capability.

## 2026-08-21: Phase 2 signed off, Phase 3 complete

Neyda approved proceeding to Phase 3 in the same session. Read `PHASE-3-HANDOFF-PROMPT.md` before starting.

**Decision 19: two CAC methods used deliberately, for different questions.** A lag-adjusted trailing window (spend shifted earlier by the segment's average sales cycle before dividing by wins in the target period) is used for the "current" blended CAC headline (Question 1). A same-period era CAC (total spend during an era divided by total wins closing in that era, no lag shift) is used for the Enterprise pre-push vs. post-push comparison (Question 2), because the lag-adjusted method would need spend history reaching back before `sm_spend`'s first row (`WINDOW_START`), which does not exist, and would silently understate the founder-era figure by truncating its spend window. Tested both; the lag-adjusted method gave a nonsensical pre-push CAC ($3,840, implying a sub-one-month payback) purely as an artifact of the missing pre-window spend data, not a real finding. The era method avoids this and is the one used for the era comparison in `docs/findings_summary.md`. Both methods and the reasoning are documented in `notebooks/02_unit_economics.ipynb`.

**Decision 20: Enterprise churn uses the locked benchmark (8%, 5-10% sensitivity); Mid-Market and SMB use empirically measured churn.** Mid-Market (24 events, 2,297 customer-months) and SMB (100 events, 3,610 customer-months) have enough observed churn to support an occurrence/exposure estimate (measured at 11.8% and 28.6% respectively, both close to their locked benchmark bands). Enterprise (6 events) does not, per the documented limitation carried since Phase 2. This was a live choice, not obvious from the handoff docs, and is logged here since a future session extending this analysis should know why the three segments use different churn sources.

**Decision 21: the EU competitor confounder required a second test method to be measured honestly.** The straightforward test (Enterprise pre/post-push decline by region) found essentially zero EU-specific excess decline, because splitting at `ENTERPRISE_PUSH_DATE` dilutes the signal: many "post-push" EU deals were opened before the competitor's actual entry date (`COMPETITOR_EU_ENTRY_DATE`, 2025-07-01), five months later. A difference-in-differences test, restricted to the post-push cohort only and split at the competitor's actual entry date, found a real if small and not-statistically-significant signal (-9.0pp EU-specific, n=30/42).
